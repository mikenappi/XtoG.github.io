---
title: Presupposing inexplicability
---

To presuppose inexplicability here is to assume the very absence of explanation that the claim of bruteness requires you to establish.

Consider the answer “This fact is brute because it is inexplicable.” If *inexplicable* means that no sufficient explanation exists, the reason simply repeats the definition of a [brute fact](../brute-fact/). If it means that nobody knows the explanation, the reason establishes only a limit on what is known. In neither reading does it supply independent grounds for the claim.
