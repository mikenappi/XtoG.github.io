---
title: The final conclusion
---

If [contingent existence](../../terms/contingent-existence/) needs an external [ground](../../terms/ground/), dependent reality cannot fully ground itself, and only [intentional selection](../../terms/intentional-selection/) can sufficiently explain contingent actualization, then contingent reality has a necessary mental foundation.

## What each stage contributes

Dependence supplies the demand for an external explanation. The totality argument places at least one ground outside the range of dependent things. The modal argument gives that ground [necessary existence](../../terms/necessary-existence/). The selection argument attributes to the ultimate explanation a capacity to represent and settle alternatives.

## What remains open

“Mental” here names a specific capacity. Identifying this foundation with God in a richer theological sense would require further arguments about uniqueness, knowledge, power, and goodness. A reader can accept the necessary-ground result and still dispute the later inference to mentality.
