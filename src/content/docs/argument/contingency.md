---
title: Contingency and dependence
---

A particular tree exists. It could have failed to exist had the seed never germinated. That judgment concerns what was possible. Saying that the tree depends on something concerns what explains its actual existence. The transition between those claims needs an argument.

## P2 · From possibility to dependence

> If a thing's existence is entirely grounded in its own intrinsic nature, then it exists necessarily.

Suppose the nature fully accounts for the thing's existence. What could vary so that this very thing failed to exist? If some further condition had to obtain before there was such a thing, that condition would share the explanatory work. On the proposed principle, possible absence therefore rules out a wholly intrinsic sufficient ground.

This treats “exists” as a claim about whether there is an instance. No step adds existence to a list of descriptive properties. The disputed issue is whether an intrinsic explanation of *actual existence* would suffice across possible circumstances.

## P5 · Why appeal to sufficient reason?

There is still a third possibility to address. Something whose existence lacks an intrinsic explanation might simply exist as a brute fact. The restricted PSR rules this out for contingent existence. Together, P2 and P5 yield the first result: whatever exists contingently requires a ground beyond itself.
