title: Objections and replies
---

## Brute existence

**Challenge to P5.** If explanation must end somewhere, why cannot it end with a contingent fact? The fact that we ordinarily seek explanations does not guarantee that one always exists.

**Reply.** Consider two otherwise similar contingent existents. If one calls for an explanation while the other is exempt, what grounds the exemption? A principled difference could justify the boundary. Merely declaring the second fact ultimate gives no reason for treating the cases differently. This puts pressure on selective exemptions. Someone who consistently accepts brute facts can still reject the restricted PSR and accept that explanatory cost.

## The dependent totality

**Challenge to P8–P10.** Explaining every member may exhaust what needs explanation. Asking for another ground can sound like counting the collection twice.

**Reply.** Grant every member its local explanation. Those explanations tell us how one member can exist given others. The collective question asks why the condition they all draw on obtains at all. No extra object need be postulated to ask it. The disagreement concerns whether the collective question adds a genuine explanatory demand. An opponent who denies that demand can accept every local relation without accepting the conclusion.

## Independent but contingent

**Challenge to P12.** “Nothing else grounds it” and “it could not have failed to exist” express different claims. Why could the independent ground not be a brute contingent existent?

**Reply.** Assume it were both independent and contingent. P2 excludes a wholly intrinsic sufficient explanation. P5 requires a sufficient explanation. Its ground must then be external, contrary to the assumption. This is a conditional proof whose force tracks the defenses of P2 and P5. Defining independence more emphatically would add nothing.

## Impersonal chance

**Challenge to P19.** A process can fully account for an event by giving its conditions and objective chances. Why demand that an explanation also distinguish the actual outcome?

**Reply.** The process supplies the same distribution in circumstances with different outcomes. If the explanandum includes why this alternative became actual, that shared distribution leaves the contrast open. The objection succeeds against anyone who silently shifts from explaining the distribution to explaining its individual realization. A defender must justify the stronger demand at the level of ultimate explanation.

## Selection and reasons

**Challenge to P20–P21.** If an agent's reasons necessitate a choice, contingency disappears. If those reasons leave the choice open, why is the remaining difference any more intelligible than chance?

**Reply.** On the proposed account, the agent's exercise of a capacity settles the outcome, and the end pursued explains what the agent saw in that option. A chance distribution as such has no end in view. The remaining dispute is whether the exercise itself requires a further contrastive ground. Imposing that demand on chance while exempting a choice without argument would undermine the inference. The mental conclusion depends on defending this asymmetry.
