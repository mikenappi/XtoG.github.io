title: Formal logical proof
---

This reconstruction separates deductions from the substantive assumptions that license them. Letters denote predicates of actual existents. **C** means contingent, **N** necessary, **S** wholly self-grounded, and **E** externally grounded.

## Lemma 1 · Contingency entails dependence

1. For every x, S(x) → N(x). Modal-to-ontological bridge.
2. For every x, C(x) → ¬N(x). Modal definitions.
3. For every x, C(x) → (S(x) ∨ E(x)). Restricted PSR with the exhaustive distinction between wholly intrinsic grounds and grounds involving something external.
4. Assume C(a). By 2, ¬N(a). By 1 and modus tollens, ¬S(a).
5. By 3 and disjunctive syllogism, E(a). Discharging the assumption yields C(x) → E(x).

## Lemma 2 · An independent ground exists necessarily

6. Dependent reality exists and has a complete ground. This applies the explanatory demand to the collective fact.
7. A complete ground of dependent reality cannot consist solely of dependent existents. This is the totality premise.
8. By 6 and 7, a complete ground includes an independent existent, g.
9. Independence gives ¬E(g). By Lemma 1, ¬C(g). Given that g exists and the modal alternatives are exhaustive, N(g).

## Lemma 3 · The mental conclusion

10. The actual order is contingent and its ultimate ground accounts for which order is actual.
11. Necessary impersonal determination would make that order necessary. Impersonal indeterminism, by P19, lacks a sufficient contrastive explanation.
12. Every exclusively impersonal ultimate explanation falls under those alternatives. By 10–11, neither suffices.
13. By P20–P21, settling the alternatives requires intentional selection by the ultimate ground, which entails mental capacity.
14. If the independent ground identified in Lemma 2 is what ultimately settles the order in 10–13, it is both necessary and mental.

Step 14 makes the identification assumption explicit. The existence of some necessary contributor and some mental contributor would be insufficient by themselves.
