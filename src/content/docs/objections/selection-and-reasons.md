---
title: "Only intentional selection can settle which order obtains while preserving the possibility of a different order."
---

**Challenge to P20–P21.** If an agent's reasons necessitate a choice, contingency disappears. If those reasons leave the choice open, why is the remaining difference any more intelligible than chance?

**Reply.** On the proposed account, the agent's exercise of a capacity settles the outcome, and the end pursued explains what the agent saw in that option. A chance distribution as such has no end in view. The remaining dispute is whether the exercise itself requires a further contrastive [ground](../../terms/ground--objections-selection-and-reasons-1/). Imposing that demand on chance while exempting a choice without argument would undermine the inference. The mental conclusion depends on defending this asymmetry.
