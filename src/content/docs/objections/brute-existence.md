---
title: Brute existence
---

**Challenge to P5.** If explanation must end somewhere, why cannot it end with a contingent fact? The fact that we ordinarily seek explanations does not guarantee that one always exists.

**Reply.** Consider two otherwise similar contingent existents. If one calls for an explanation while the other is exempt, what grounds the exemption? A principled difference could justify the boundary. Merely declaring the second fact ultimate gives no reason for treating the cases differently. This puts pressure on selective exemptions. Someone who consistently accepts brute facts can still reject the restricted PSR and accept that explanatory cost.
